# fulcrum

[![ci](https://github.com/whoiam1101/fulcrum/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/whoiam1101/fulcrum/actions/workflows/ci.yml)
[![codeql](https://github.com/whoiam1101/fulcrum/actions/workflows/codeql.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/whoiam1101/fulcrum/actions/workflows/codeql.yml)
[![license](https://img.shields.io/badge/license-Apache--2.0-blue.svg)](LICENSE)
[![msrv](https://img.shields.io/badge/MSRV-1.60-orange.svg)](Cargo.toml)

fulcrum is a deterministic fixed-point math library for Rust. Every operation, including sqrt, sin, cos and atan2, gives bit-identical results on x86-64 and ARM64, with no floating point used internally. Overflow is explicit and never panics. It includes 2D/3D vectors and stable serialization for snapshots, replays and checksums. It is no_std and suited to lockstep networking, replays and any simulation that must agree across machines.

> **Status:** early development. The architecture is in [`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md); the API is not implemented yet.

## Benchmarks

Measured on 2026-11-06, commit `acc8cb9`, local machine: Intel(R) Core(TM) 7 240H, x86_64 linux, rustc 1.99.1 (58cf8f9c3 2026-10-13).

Generated by `bench/src/bin/report.rs` from [`bench/benches/ops.rs`](bench/benches/ops.rs). Operands in `[-2, 2)`, divisors in `±[0.5, 2)`. *Throughput*: 4096 independent operations, the compiler may vectorize. *Latency*: a dependent chain, each result feeds the next operation.

### Summary

- **Against `fixed`** (same formats, saturating operators): fulcrum is faster in 8 of 15 cases, on par in 0, slower in 7.
  - `+`: out of 3 cases, faster in 2 (1.06–1.18×), slower in 1 (1.15×).
  - `*`: out of 6 cases, faster in 5 (1.21–6.29×), slower in 1 (1.38×).
  - `/`: out of 6 cases, faster in 1 (1.38×), slower in 5 (1.13–1.97×).
- **Biggest win:** `*`, throughput of `FixedFloat16`, 6.29× faster than `fixed` I8F8.
- **Biggest loss:** `/`, throughput of `FixedFloat16`, 1.97× slower than `fixed` I8F8.
- **Cost of determinism**, `FixedFloat32` vs hardware `f32`: `+`, throughput 2.78× slower; `*`, throughput 7.50× slower; `*`, latency 1.28× slower; `/`, throughput 18.86× slower; `/`, latency 2.56× slower.
- **Cost of determinism**, `FixedFloat64` vs hardware `f64`: `+`, throughput 2.54× slower; `*`, throughput 2.93× slower; `*`, latency 3.75× slower; `/`, throughput 6.19× slower; `/`, latency 2.68× slower.

### How to read this

- A difference is reported only when the 95% confidence intervals of the two medians do not overlap and the medians differ by at least 5%; everything else is *on par*. Absolute numbers depend on the runner's CPU, ratios measured in the same run are what to compare.
- `fixed` is measured with `saturating_*`, the closest to fulcrum semantics. fulcrum additionally rounds `*` and `/` to nearest, while `fixed` truncates: fulcrum does more work per operation and returns a more accurate result.
- `f32`/`f64` are the speed ceiling: hardware floats are fast, but their results may differ between platforms and compilers. The gap shows the price of bit-identical results.
- Reproduce locally: `cd bench && cargo bench --bench ops && cargo run --release --bin report`.
