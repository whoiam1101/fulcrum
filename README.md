Deterministic fixed-point math for Rust: bit-identical results on every platform, no floating point inside. Operators saturate and never panic; sums are exact and independent of order and thread count. `no_std`, no required dependencies, MSRV 1.60. Made for lockstep networking, replays and simulations that must agree across machines.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Formally verified in Lean 4.** The arithmetic kernel `src/kernel.rs`, which owns every width and every rounding of the crate, is proven correct on the code itself, not on a model: Charon and Aeneas translate the Rust to Lean, and the proofs show that rounding, narrowing, saturation, `*`, `/`, `div_int`, `mul_add`, `mul_div`, `lerp`, rescaling between scales and the integer square root of `FixedFloat8` … `FixedFloat64` never panic and return exactly the specified result — rounded to nearest, ties toward +∞ — for every input of their contract (a non-zero divisor, the root on its domain). The proofs use no axiom beyond those of Lean and Mathlib, no `sorry` and no `native_decide`; CI re-extracts the kernel, rebuilds the proofs and replays them through the Lean kernel on every push. See [`verify/`](verify/).

Types: `FixedFloat8` (Q4.4), `FixedFloat16` (Q8.8), `FixedFloat32` (Q16.16), `FixedFloat64` (Q32.32); vectors `Vec2<T>`, `Vec3<T>` over any of them.

> **Status:** stages 1–7 of the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) are done: arithmetic, `mul_add`, `mul_div`, `lerp`, exact accumulators, correctly rounded `sqrt`, `rsqrt`, `hypot`; trigonometry within 1 ulp (`sin_turns`, `cos_turns` and, in radians, `sin`, `cos`, `sin_cos`, `tan`, `atan`, `atan2`, `asin`, `acos`) and the wrapping angle types `Angle8` … `Angle64`; the vectors `Vec2` and `Vec3` (`dot`, `cross`, lengths rounded once, correctly rounded `normalize`, `rotate`, `angle_to`); `log2`, `exp2`, `ln`, `exp` within ½ ulp plus a small margin, and `pow`, `powi` within 1 ulp; plain loops over slices of 8–32 bits that vectorize on SSE2, AVX2 and NEON, checked in CI; optional `serde`, `bytemuck` and `num-traits` support. The API may change before 1.0. Not on crates.io; use a git dependency pinned to a tag.

```toml
[dependencies]
fulcrum = { git = "https://github.com/whoiam1101/fulcrum", tag = "v0.7.1" }
```

## Example

```rust
use fulcrum::{ff64, FixedFloat64};

const SPEED: FixedFloat64 = ff64!(12.6);

let dt: FixedFloat64 = "0.016".parse().unwrap();
let step = SPEED * dt;
assert_eq!(format!("{:.4}", step), "0.2016");

assert_eq!(FixedFloat64::MAX + FixedFloat64::ONE, FixedFloat64::MAX);
assert_eq!(FixedFloat64::MAX.checked_add(FixedFloat64::ONE), None);

let xs = [ff64!(0.1), ff64!(0.2), ff64!(0.3)];
let mut left = FixedFloat64::accumulator();
left.extend(&xs[..1]);
let mut right = FixedFloat64::accumulator();
right.extend(&xs[1..]);
assert_eq!(left.merge(right).finish(), xs.iter().sum::<FixedFloat64>());
```

## Benchmarks

Measured on 2026-11-21, commit `177ef29`, GitHub-hosted runner: Intel(R) Xeon(R) 6975P-C, x86_64 linux, rustc 1.100.0 (0ef1bc386 2026-11-09).

Generated by `bench/src/bin/report.rs` from [`bench/benches/ops.rs`](bench/benches/ops.rs). Operands in `[-2, 2)`, divisors in `±[0.5, 2)`. *Throughput*: 4096 independent operations, the compiler may vectorize. *Latency*: a dependent chain, each result feeds the next operation.

### Summary

- **Against `fixed`** (same formats, saturating operators): fulcrum is faster in 8 of 15 cases, on par in 5, slower in 2.
  - `+`: out of 3 cases, on par in 3.
  - `*`: out of 6 cases, faster in 6 (1.22–6.44×).
  - `/`: out of 6 cases, faster in 2 (1.24–1.41×), on par in 2, slower in 2 (1.09–1.14×).
- **Biggest win:** `*`, throughput of `FixedFloat16`, 6.44× faster than `fixed` I8F8.
- **Biggest loss:** `/`, throughput of `FixedFloat16`, 1.14× slower than `fixed` I8F8.
- **Combined operations** (`mul_add`, `mul_div`, `lerp`, dot product) against `fixed`: faster in 7 of 8 cases, on par in 0, slower in 1.
- **Loops over slices** (`+`, `-`, `*`, `mul_int`, `mul_add`, `lerp` on 8, 16 and 32 bits) against `fixed`: faster in 9 of 18 cases, on par in 8, slower in 1.
- **Square roots**, `FixedFloat32` vs `libm` `f32` (fulcrum correctly rounded, floats not once stored in the format, see the accuracy table): `sqrt` 4.22× slower; `rsqrt` 3.14× slower; `hypot` 2.50× slower.
- **Square roots**, `FixedFloat64` vs `libm` `f64` (fulcrum correctly rounded, floats not once stored in the format, see the accuracy table): `sqrt` 14.91× slower; `rsqrt` 2.82× slower; `hypot` 4.70× slower.
- **Trigonometry**, `FixedFloat32`: against `libm` `f32` faster in 2 of 9 cases, on par in 0, slower in 7; against `cordic` `I16F16` faster in 5 of 7 cases, on par in 1, slower in 1 (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).
- **Exponentials and logarithms**, `FixedFloat32`: against `libm` `f32` faster in 0 of 6 cases, on par in 0, slower in 6; `exp` against `cordic` `I16F16` 1.22× faster (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).
- **Trigonometry**, `FixedFloat64`: against `libm` `f64` faster in 2 of 9 cases, on par in 1, slower in 6; against `cordic` `I32F32` faster in 7 of 7 cases, on par in 0, slower in 0 (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).

- **Cost of determinism**, `FixedFloat32` vs hardware `f32`: `+`, throughput 3.49× slower; `*`, throughput 7.25× slower; `*`, latency 1.70× slower; `/`, throughput 13.21× slower; `/`, latency 2.10× slower.

### Accuracy of trigonometry

Error in ulps of the fixed-point format against the 256-bit interval oracle, clamped to the range, over 20000 operands per row; each cell is the largest error and the share of correctly rounded results. Floats (`f32` for Q16.16, `f64` for Q32.32) get the operand exactly and their result rounded to nearest into the format; for `sin_turns` and `cos_turns` they compute `sin(τ·x)` and `cos(τ·x)`. `cordic` is the CORDIC crate on `I16F16` and `I32F32`; its `tan` divides `sin` by `cos` in `fixed`, which wraps on overflow near the poles, hence the huge errors there. Generated by [`bench/src/trig_accuracy.rs`](bench/src/trig_accuracy.rs).

### How to read this

- A difference is reported only when the 95% confidence intervals of the two medians do not overlap and the medians differ by at least 5%; everything else is *on par*. Absolute numbers depend on the runner's CPU, ratios measured in the same run are what to compare.
- `fixed` is measured with `saturating_*`, the closest to fulcrum semantics. fulcrum additionally rounds `*` and `/` to nearest, while `fixed` truncates: fulcrum does more work per operation and returns a more accurate result.
- `i32`/`i64`/`i128` are plain integer arithmetic on the same loops, without rounding or saturation: a reference point for the cost of the integer operations themselves.
- `f32`/`f64` are the speed ceiling: hardware floats are fast, but their results may differ between platforms and compilers. The gap shows the price of bit-identical results.
- Reproduce locally: `cd bench && cargo bench --bench ops && cargo run --release --bin report`.
