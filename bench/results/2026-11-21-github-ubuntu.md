# Benchmark results, 2026-11-21

Measured on 2026-11-21, commit `177ef29`, GitHub-hosted runner: Intel(R) Xeon(R) 6975P-C, x86_64 linux, rustc 1.100.0 (0ef1bc386 2026-11-09).

Generated by `bench/src/bin/report.rs` from [`bench/benches/ops.rs`](../benches/ops.rs). Operands in `[-2, 2)`, divisors in `±[0.5, 2)`. *Throughput*: 4096 independent operations, the compiler may vectorize. *Latency*: a dependent chain, each result feeds the next operation.

## Summary

- **Against `fixed`** (same formats, saturating operators): fulcrum is faster in 8 of 15 cases, on par in 5, slower in 2.
  - `+`: out of 3 cases, on par in 3.
  - `*`: out of 6 cases, faster in 6 (1.22–6.44×).
  - `/`: out of 6 cases, faster in 2 (1.24–1.41×), on par in 2, slower in 2 (1.09–1.14×).
- **Biggest win:** `*`, throughput of `FixedFloat16`, 6.44× faster than `fixed` I8F8.
- **Biggest loss:** `/`, throughput of `FixedFloat16`, 1.14× slower than `fixed` I8F8.
- **Combined operations** (`mul_add`, `mul_div`, `lerp`, dot product) against `fixed`: faster in 7 of 8 cases, on par in 0, slower in 1.
- **Loops over slices** (`+`, `-`, `*`, `mul_int`, `mul_add`, `lerp` on 8, 16 and 32 bits) against `fixed`: faster in 9 of 18 cases, on par in 8, slower in 1.
- **Square roots**, `FixedFloat32` vs `libm` `f32` (fulcrum correctly rounded, floats not once stored in the format, see the accuracy table): `sqrt` 4.22× slower; `rsqrt` 3.14× slower; `hypot` 2.50× slower.
- **Square roots**, `FixedFloat64` vs `libm` `f64` (fulcrum correctly rounded, floats not once stored in the format, see the accuracy table): `sqrt` 14.91× slower; `rsqrt` 2.82× slower; `hypot` 4.70× slower.
- **Trigonometry**, `FixedFloat32`: against `libm` `f32` faster in 2 of 9 cases, on par in 0, slower in 7; against `cordic` `I16F16` faster in 5 of 7 cases, on par in 1, slower in 1 (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).
- **Exponentials and logarithms**, `FixedFloat32`: against `libm` `f32` faster in 0 of 6 cases, on par in 0, slower in 6; `exp` against `cordic` `I16F16` 1.22× faster (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).
- **Trigonometry**, `FixedFloat64`: against `libm` `f64` faster in 2 of 9 cases, on par in 1, slower in 6; against `cordic` `I32F32` faster in 7 of 7 cases, on par in 0, slower in 0 (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).
- **Exponentials and logarithms**, `FixedFloat64`: against `libm` `f64` faster in 1 of 6 cases, on par in 0, slower in 5; `exp` against `cordic` `I32F32` 13.64× faster (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).
- **Cost of determinism**, `FixedFloat32` vs hardware `f32`: `+`, throughput 3.49× slower; `*`, throughput 7.25× slower; `*`, latency 1.70× slower; `/`, throughput 13.21× slower; `/`, latency 2.10× slower.
- **Cost of determinism**, `FixedFloat64` vs hardware `f64`: `+`, throughput 4.29× slower; `*`, throughput 5.77× slower; `*`, latency 2.77× slower; `/`, throughput 5.49× slower; `/`, latency 1.93× slower.

## How to read this

- A difference is reported only when the 95% confidence intervals of the two medians do not overlap and the medians differ by at least 5%; everything else is *on par*. Absolute numbers depend on the runner's CPU, ratios measured in the same run are what to compare.
- `fixed` is measured with `saturating_*`, the closest to fulcrum semantics. fulcrum additionally rounds `*` and `/` to nearest, while `fixed` truncates: fulcrum does more work per operation and returns a more accurate result.
- `i32`/`i64`/`i128` are plain integer arithmetic on the same loops, without rounding or saturation: a reference point for the cost of the integer operations themselves.
- `f32`/`f64` are the speed ceiling: hardware floats are fast, but their results may differ between platforms and compilers. The gap shows the price of bit-identical results.
- Reproduce locally: `cd bench && cargo bench --bench ops && cargo run --release --bin report`.
