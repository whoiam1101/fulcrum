# Benchmark results, 2026-11-18

Measured on 2026-11-18, commit `d191f37`, GitHub-hosted runner: AMD EPYC 9V74 80-Core Processor, x86_64 linux, rustc 1.99.1 (58cf8f9c3 2026-10-13).

Generated by `bench/src/bin/report.rs` from [`bench/benches/ops.rs`](../benches/ops.rs). Operands in `[-2, 2)`, divisors in `±[0.5, 2)`. *Throughput*: 4096 independent operations, the compiler may vectorize. *Latency*: a dependent chain, each result feeds the next operation.

## Summary

- **Against `fixed`** (same formats, saturating operators): fulcrum is faster in 8 of 15 cases, on par in 4, slower in 3.
  - `+`: out of 3 cases, on par in 3.
  - `*`: out of 6 cases, faster in 6 (1.33–6.19×).
  - `/`: out of 6 cases, faster in 2 (1.10–1.48×), on par in 1, slower in 3 (1.06–1.30×).
- **Biggest win:** `*`, throughput of `FixedFloat16`, 6.19× faster than `fixed` I8F8.
- **Biggest loss:** `/`, throughput of `FixedFloat16`, 1.30× slower than `fixed` I8F8.
- **Combined operations** (`mul_add`, `mul_div`, `lerp`, dot product) against `fixed`: faster in 6 of 8 cases, on par in 0, slower in 2.
- **Square roots**, `FixedFloat32` vs `libm` `f32` (fulcrum correctly rounded, floats not once stored in the format, see the accuracy table): `sqrt` 3.10× slower; `rsqrt` 2.80× slower; `hypot` 3.13× slower.
- **Square roots**, `FixedFloat64` vs `libm` `f64` (fulcrum correctly rounded, floats not once stored in the format, see the accuracy table): `sqrt` 10.31× slower; `rsqrt` 3.00× slower; `hypot` 4.60× slower.
- **Trigonometry**, `FixedFloat32`: against `libm` `f32` faster in 0 of 9, on par in 0, slower in 9; against `cordic` `I16F16` faster in 5 of 7, on par in 0, slower in 2 (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).
- **Exponentials and logarithms**, `FixedFloat32`: against `libm` `f32` faster in 0 of 6, on par in 0, slower in 6; `exp` against `cordic` `I16F16` 1.16× faster (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).
- **Trigonometry**, `FixedFloat64`: against `libm` `f64` faster in 0 of 9, on par in 2, slower in 7; against `cordic` `I32F32` faster in 7 of 7, on par in 0, slower in 0 (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).
- **Exponentials and logarithms**, `FixedFloat64`: against `libm` `f64` faster in 0 of 6, on par in 0, slower in 6; `exp` against `cordic` `I32F32` 10.43× faster (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).
- **Cost of determinism**, `FixedFloat32` vs hardware `f32`: `+`, throughput 1.83× slower; `*`, throughput 7.29× slower; `*`, latency 1.69× slower; `/`, throughput 10.22× slower; `/`, latency 1.75× slower.
- **Cost of determinism**, `FixedFloat64` vs hardware `f64`: `+`, throughput 3.62× slower; `*`, throughput 9.87× slower; `*`, latency 2.39× slower; `/`, throughput 5.51× slower; `/`, latency 1.91× slower.

## How to read this

- A difference is reported only when the 95% confidence intervals of the two medians do not overlap and the medians differ by at least 5%; everything else is *on par*. Absolute numbers depend on the runner's CPU, ratios measured in the same run are what to compare.
- `fixed` is measured with `saturating_*`, the closest to fulcrum semantics. fulcrum additionally rounds `*` and `/` to nearest, while `fixed` truncates: fulcrum does more work per operation and returns a more accurate result.
- `i32`/`i64`/`i128` are plain integer arithmetic on the same loops, without rounding or saturation: a reference point for the cost of the integer operations themselves.
- `f32`/`f64` are the speed ceiling: hardware floats are fast, but their results may differ between platforms and compilers. The gap shows the price of bit-identical results.
- Reproduce locally: `cd bench && cargo bench --bench ops && cargo run --release --bin report`.
