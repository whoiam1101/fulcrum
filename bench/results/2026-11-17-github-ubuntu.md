# Benchmark results, 2026-11-17

Measured on 2026-11-17, commit `0996954`, GitHub-hosted runner: AMD EPYC 9V45 96-Core Processor, x86_64 linux, rustc 1.99.1 (58cf8f9c3 2026-10-13).

Generated by `bench/src/bin/report.rs` from [`bench/benches/ops.rs`](../benches/ops.rs). Operands in `[-2, 2)`, divisors in `±[0.5, 2)`. *Throughput*: 4096 independent operations, the compiler may vectorize. *Latency*: a dependent chain, each result feeds the next operation.

## Summary

- **Against `fixed`** (same formats, saturating operators): fulcrum is faster in 9 of 15 cases, on par in 2, slower in 4.
  - `+`: out of 3 cases, faster in 1 (1.09×), on par in 2.
  - `*`: out of 6 cases, faster in 6 (1.17–9.73×).
  - `/`: out of 6 cases, faster in 2 (1.18–1.36×), slower in 4 (1.07–1.26×).
- **Biggest win:** `*`, throughput of `FixedFloat16`, 9.73× faster than `fixed` I8F8.
- **Biggest loss:** `/`, throughput of `FixedFloat16`, 1.26× slower than `fixed` I8F8.
- **Combined operations** (`mul_add`, `mul_div`, `lerp`, dot product) against `fixed`: faster in 7 of 8 cases, on par in 0, slower in 1.
- **Square roots**, `FixedFloat32` vs `libm` `f32` (fulcrum correctly rounded, floats not once stored in the format, see the accuracy table): `sqrt` 3.38× slower; `rsqrt` 3.27× slower; `hypot` 4.21× slower.
- **Square roots**, `FixedFloat64` vs `libm` `f64` (fulcrum correctly rounded, floats not once stored in the format, see the accuracy table): `sqrt` 11.36× slower; `rsqrt` 3.60× slower; `hypot` 5.64× slower.
- **Trigonometry**, `FixedFloat32`: against `libm` `f32` faster in 0 of 9, on par in 0, slower in 9; against `cordic` `I16F16` faster in 5 of 7, on par in 0, slower in 2 (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).
- **Trigonometry**, `FixedFloat64`: against `libm` `f64` faster in 2 of 9, on par in 0, slower in 7; against `cordic` `I32F32` faster in 7 of 7, on par in 0, slower in 0 (fulcrum within 1 ulp, see the accuracy table).
- **Cost of determinism**, `FixedFloat32` vs hardware `f32`: `+`, throughput 2.27× slower; `*`, throughput 9.28× slower; `*`, latency 1.71× slower; `/`, throughput 12.83× slower; `/`, latency 2.12× slower.
- **Cost of determinism**, `FixedFloat64` vs hardware `f64`: `+`, throughput 4.24× slower; `*`, throughput 9.88× slower; `*`, latency 2.71× slower; `/`, throughput 6.29× slower; `/`, latency 1.99× slower.

## How to read this

- A difference is reported only when the 95% confidence intervals of the two medians do not overlap and the medians differ by at least 5%; everything else is *on par*. Absolute numbers depend on the runner's CPU, ratios measured in the same run are what to compare.
- `fixed` is measured with `saturating_*`, the closest to fulcrum semantics. fulcrum additionally rounds `*` and `/` to nearest, while `fixed` truncates: fulcrum does more work per operation and returns a more accurate result.
- `i32`/`i64`/`i128` are plain integer arithmetic on the same loops, without rounding or saturation: a reference point for the cost of the integer operations themselves.
- `f32`/`f64` are the speed ceiling: hardware floats are fast, but their results may differ between platforms and compilers. The gap shows the price of bit-identical results.
- Reproduce locally: `cd bench && cargo bench --bench ops && cargo run --release --bin report`.
